# Design context

Written from what the owner has said across the build of this app, not inferred
from the code. Update it when they say something that contradicts it.

## Design Context

### Users

One person and their colleagues, all on current macOS, all speaking Ukrainian
with English technical vocabulary mixed in. They are engineers and managers who
are in calls most of the day and have no time to press record.

The app is open beside a call, or opened afterwards to find out what was agreed.
The two questions it exists to answer are "what did I miss" and "what did I say
I would do". It runs all day in the background, so it must never demand
attention it has not earned.

### Brand personality

**Quiet, exact, unhurried.** It is a piece of instrumentation, not a product
that wants to be liked. It states what it knows, says plainly when it does not
know, and never celebrates.

Nothing in it is chatty. No exclamation marks, no "Oops!", no encouragement.
Errors say what happened and what to do. Every wait has a name — "listening",
"writing it down", "reading it back" — because a pause with no name reads as
broken.

### Aesthetic direction

Dark, and not as a default: the app lives beside a video call in a room where
the lights are off half the year, and a white panel next to a call window is a
lamp pointed at the face.

Violet is the app's colour and stays. The rest is no longer anybody's guess: on
2026-09-08 the owner went through three hundred swatches and marked thirty he
wants, striking out magenta, raspberry and pink (320–350), turquoise and cyan
(185–215) and lemon (90) entirely. His most frequent pick was L 78% / C 0.19,
which is what identity colours now use. See the memory note, and
`mockups/palette.html` if the list needs extending.

Around violet the neutrals are cool and nearly
achromatic — chroma 0.008–0.015 at hue 268 — which is what makes one saturated
accent read as saturated. The status colours are pushed to the edge of P3
(coral 22, spring green 152, cyan 205) after the owner said the earlier muted
set looked lifeless. Per-speaker colours are the same family, high chroma,
spread across the wheel.

Type is the system stack. This is a macOS tool and it should look like one;
a display face here would be costume.

The owner asked for "реально крутий продакшн рівня інтерфейс з усілякими
анімаціями, ефектами, у сучасному стилі" — so motion is expected, but it is
motion that explains a change, never decoration. Anything that repeats for ever
is a bug waiting to happen (see the AnimatePresence note in PLAN.md).

**Anti-references:** the coral "Delete for ever" pill that appeared on hover
over a card's timestamp. The owner's exact words were that it looks bad and so
does the way it appears. It is the shape of everything to avoid: a destructive
action given the loudest treatment on the screen, revealed by hover so it cannot
be reached by keyboard, and positioned on top of content it obscures.

What replaced it is the pattern to follow: a trailing action column that keeps
its width whether or not it is showing, revealed by hover *or* keyboard focus,
and a single quiet click that moves the recording to a bin with ten seconds of
undo. No dialog, nothing covered, nothing that moves.

### Design principles

1. **Destructive actions are quiet and reversible.** Prefer undo to
   confirmation. A bin the user can look in beats a dialog they have to read.
2. **Nothing appears on top of content.** An action that only exists on hover
   still occupies its space when hidden, so nothing moves and nothing is
   covered.
3. **Hover is an enhancement, never the only way in.** Keyboard focus reveals
   the same things hover does.
4. **The interface states facts, not opinions.** It shows that one person spoke
   for 62% of a meeting; it does not say they talked too much.
5. **Less on the screen.** When two layouts both work, the one with fewer
   boxes, fewer borders and fewer words wins.
