## Design Context

### Users
People who sat through a meeting and now have to get something out of the recording:
find the decision, check who said what, correct a misheard name, hand a summary to
someone who missed it. They work on a laptop, in daylight or late evening, and they
read long — an hour of talk is a thousand turns of text. A second audience is the
person who was handed a share link and only wants to read.

### Brand Personality
Attentive, procedural, unhurried. The tool behaves like a court reporter: it listens
carefully, records faithfully, timestamps everything, and never pretends to be
finished before it is. Nothing happens silently and nothing jumps around on its own.

### Aesthetic Direction
A reading instrument, not a dashboard. Archivo carries the furniture — labels,
figures, controls — with tabular numerals so timestamps line up in a column.
Literata carries the words people actually read: transcript turns, summaries,
answers. That split is the whole idea made visible — chrome versus document.

Dark is the room this is used in, so dark is designed first: a near-black ground
with a violet whisper in it, near-white type, and one vivid violet accent spent
sparingly. Surfaces step up in lightness — ground, card, raised — so hierarchy comes
from depth rather than from paint. No large field is ever filled with the accent; a
summary is a card with accent labels, not a coloured slab. That was the mistake in
the first pass, along with muddy blue-grey grounds and low-contrast type.

The palette is OKLCH, light and dark defined as one set of variables so no `dark:`
variant appears in the markup. Red belongs to recording alone. Eight speaker hues sit
at one lightness so no voice looks louder than another.

Anti-references: indigo-on-slate SaaS default, glassmorphism, cards inside cards,
big rounded icons above every heading.

### Design Principles
1. **A tab does its own job.** Library shows transcripts, Live listens, Upload
   transcribes. Nothing navigates on the user's behalf.
2. **Say what is happening.** Warming up, listening, still transcribing, saved —
   each has a visible state. A wait the user cannot see is a bug.
3. **The transcript is a document.** Sticky transport, timeline, search and
   auto-follow, because it is read for an hour, not glanced at.
4. **Reach for a token, not a variant.** One semantic palette, no `dark:` classes.
5. **Less markup wins.** If a design needs a wrapper to work, it is the wrong design.
